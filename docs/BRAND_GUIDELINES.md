# Realm of Qimen brand guidelines

## Brand idea

Realm of Qimen presents traditional Chinese metaphysics in a calm, practical, and approachable way. The experience should feel thoughtful and refined without becoming theatrical, overly mystical, clinical, or intimidating.

**Positioning line:** Traditional wisdom for modern life.

**Voice:** Warm, clear, grounded, respectful, encouraging, and human.

## Logo

Use the supplied Realm of Qimen logo without redrawing, recolouring, stretching, cropping, or replacing its lettering. Give it clear space and use it on a warm light background whenever possible.

The lettering inside the logo is treated as part of the artwork. It is not an additional website typeface.

## Typography

Use no more than two type families across the website and generated communications.

### Display type

- Family: Georgia, with Times New Roman as fallback.
- Use for page titles, section headings, card titles, and report headings.
- Use regular weight.
- Avoid long passages and uppercase paragraphs.

### Body and interface type

- Family: Arial, with Helvetica as fallback.
- Use for paragraphs, forms, navigation, buttons, captions, data, and legal text.
- Use regular weight for body copy and bold only for clear emphasis.

### Labels

- Use the body typeface in uppercase.
- Use bold weight with generous letter spacing.
- Keep labels short; they should guide the reader rather than compete with headings.

## Layout and imagery

- Use generous space, clear reading order, and simple rectangular cards.
- Prefer fine borders and pale backgrounds over heavy shadows.
- Keep mobile headings restrained so the content remains easy to scan.
- Use the Bazi, Qimen, and Feng Shui icons as one teal line-art family.
- Metaphorical imagery should clarify an idea, never decorate for its own sake.

## Writing

- Explain specialist terms in familiar language when they first appear.
- Write so a 12-year-old can understand each sentence on the first reading.
- Use concrete examples and clear subjects.
- Avoid mechanical phrasing, exaggerated promises, superstition, certainty, diagnosis, and fear-based language.
- Address people as individuals rather than charts, subjects, or profiles.
